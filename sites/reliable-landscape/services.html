What We Install & Maintain

Everything that waters, grows, or holds water on the property.

Sprinkler installations and repair, seamless sod, hydroseeding, landscape design and maintenance, tree pruning, and water features. Those are the six things Reliable Landscape and Irrigation lists as its own work, handled by one crew out of Rigby.

Six Services

Each one its own craft, planned as one system.

A property is a small water-and-growth system. Below is what each service actually involves when it goes in the ground.

Sprinkler installations and repair

Zoned irrigation designed before it is dug: head placement, arc overlap, valve grouping, and controller programming worked out so every zone lands water evenly instead of leaving dry corners and washing gravel into the street. Repair work is the other half of it. A system that was installed right ten years ago still gets a cracked lateral, a head sheared off by a mower, a valve that will not close, or a zone that lost pressure after a hard Rigby winter.

  • New installs
  • Zone repairs
  • Valves & controllers
  • Head replacement

Seamless sod installation

A finished lawn on the day the last roll goes down. The work that decides whether it looks seamless happens first: grading so water runs where it should, soil prep so roots have something to take into, and tight staggered seams so the joints disappear once it knits. Sod is the right call when a lawn is needed now rather than after a season of watering bare seed.

  • Grading
  • Soil prep
  • Staggered seams

Hydroseeding

Seed, mulch, and tackifier sprayed as a single slurry over prepared ground. The mulch holds moisture against the seed and the tackifier keeps the whole layer from moving in the wind, which is what makes it work on the large or sloped acreage around here where sod is not practical or not worth the cost.

  • Acreage
  • Slopes
  • Seed, mulch & tackifier

Landscape design and maintenance

Beds, borders, rock work, and planting laid out as a plan rather than added one weekend at a time. Maintenance is the part that decides whether the plan survives its third year: keeping beds edged, plantings shaped, and the irrigation tuned to what the plants have grown into rather than what they were when they went in.

  • Planting plans
  • Beds & borders
  • Ongoing maintenance

Tree pruning

Structural pruning on the trees already in the yard: clearing deadwood, opening a crowded canopy so light reaches the lawn underneath, and taking weight off limbs before a wet spring snow finds them. Cuts made in the right place heal; cuts made in the wrong place leave a wound the tree spends years working around.

  • Deadwood removal
  • Canopy thinning
  • Structural cuts

Water features

Ponds, constructed creeks, and boulder features built into the grade rather than set on top of it. Reliable's own album calls two of these "Natural Creek Canal", a watercourse dug, lined, and edged in river boulder so the water reads as though it was always running there. Plumbing, liner, and drainage are decided alongside the irrigation plan, not bolted on afterward.

  • Ponds
  • Constructed creeks
  • Boulder edging
How A Job Runs

Drawn first, dug second.

The order matters more than any single component. Coverage, grade, and drainage get settled on paper, because a head in the wrong place is cheap to move on a drawing and expensive to move once the trench is backfilled.

  1. 1

    Walk the property

    Grade, water source, existing lines, sun and shade, and what the ground actually does when it rains.

  2. 2

    Draw the zones

    Head placement and arc overlap worked out per zone so coverage is even and nothing is watering the driveway.

  3. 3

    Trench, plumb, and set

    Mainline, laterals, valves, and heads installed to the plan, with the grade corrected before anything green goes down.

  4. 4

    Sod, seed, or plant

    Sod laid, hydroseed sprayed, or beds planted onto ground that is already graded and already watered correctly.
