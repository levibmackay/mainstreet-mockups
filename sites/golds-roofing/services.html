Four kinds of work

Most calls fall into one of these. If yours doesn't, call anyway and describe it.

Roof Replacement

Full tear-off down to the deck, then a complete re-roof using GAF and CertainTeed certified systems. Tearing off matters: it is the only way to see the decking, find soft spots, and set the underlayment and flashings on sound wood instead of burying a problem under a second layer.

Roofs in this valley carry snow load for months and then get hit by spring freeze-thaw, so the parts nobody sees from the ground (ice and water shield at the eaves, valleys done properly, ridge ventilation) are what decide how long the roof lasts.

  • Full tear-off
  • Deck inspection
  • Architectural shingles
  • Ridge ventilation

Roof Repair

Leaks, missing or lifted shingles, failed flashing around chimneys, pipes and sidewalls, and ice-dam damage along the eaves. A repair starts with finding where the water actually enters, which is often several feet uphill from the stain on the ceiling.

Not every roof needs replacing. If a repair will hold, we'll say so.

  • Leaks
  • Flashing
  • Ice dams
  • Wind-lifted shingles

Storm Damage

Wind, hail and snow-load assessment after a storm goes through, with the damage documented so it can support an insurance claim. Hail bruising in particular is easy to miss from the driveway and easy to prove once somebody is on the roof looking for it.

If you think something moved during a storm, get it looked at before the next one.

  • Wind
  • Hail
  • Snow load
  • Claim documentation

Roof Inspections

An honest look at what the roof is actually doing: what needs attention now, what can wait a season, and roughly how much life is left in it. Useful before you buy a house, before you sell one, and after any storm you weren't home for.

You get told what we found, not a sales pitch.

  • Post-storm
  • Annual check
Anatomy of a re-roof

A roof is a system, not a stack of shingles.

When people compare roofing bids they usually compare the shingle. The shingle is the part you can see. Everything under it is the part that decides whether water gets into the house, and it is where the difference between a certified install and a cheap one actually lives.

  1. Layer 1

    Roof deck

    The plywood or OSB the whole assembly is fastened to. Soft or rotted sheets get replaced here, while they are visible.

  2. Layer 2

    Ice & water shield

    Self-adhering membrane at the eaves and valleys, where meltwater backs up behind ice in a Rexburg-to-Ashton winter.

  3. Layer 3

    Underlayment

    The full-field water barrier over the rest of the deck. The last line of defence if a shingle ever lifts.

  4. Layer 4

    Starter course

    The first sealed row along the eave and rakes. Wind damage usually starts here when it is skipped.

  5. Layer 5

    Architectural shingles

    The visible field. Heavier and more dimensional than a three-tab, and rated for higher wind uplift.

  6. Layer 6

    Ridge ventilation

    Continuous venting along the ridge and hips. It is the run of vents you can see along the ridge in the photos on our home page.
