Under a paver patio

Four layers, and you only ever see one.

People compare patio bids on the paver. The paver is the cheapest decision in the whole job. What decides whether the patio is still flat in ten years is the three layers under it and how well they were compacted.

  1. Layer 1

    Pavers

    The visible surface, set tight with swept joints and held at the perimeter by an edge restraint so the field cannot creep.

  2. Layer 2

    Bedding sand

    A thin, screeded setting layer. Thin on purpose: a deep sand bed is one of the most common reasons a patio goes wavy.

  3. Layer 3

    Compacted road base

    The structural layer, built up and compacted in lifts. This is the part that carries the load and the part cheap jobs skimp on.

  4. Layer 4

    Prepared subgrade

    Excavated down to firm ground and graded to drain. Idaho frost heave starts here when this is done poorly.

Why sprinkler layout matters

A dry ring in July is a design problem, not a watering problem.

Turning the timer up does not fix a lawn that was laid out with gaps in it. It just drowns the parts that were already getting water and runs your bill up.

Head to head spacing means every head throws far enough to reach the next one, so the weak outer edge of one spray pattern lands on top of the strong inner part of another. It costs a bit more in heads at install and it is the difference between a lawn that is even and one that is patchy for its whole life.
